A Culinary Legacy

The History of
Kacchi Biriyani

From the royal kitchens of the Mughals to the iconic lanes of Old Dhaka — a dish that has stood the test of time.

"Kacchi Biriyani is not just a recipe — it is a ritual. A slow ceremony of spice, patience, and tradition."

What is Kacchi Biriyani?

Kacchi Biriyani — often called Kacchi Gosht Ki Biryani — is a method of cooking where raw (kacchi) marinated meat and uncooked rice are layered together and slow-cooked simultaneously in a sealed pot. This is what makes it fundamentally different from all other biryani styles, where the meat is pre-cooked.

The result is a dish where every grain of rice is infused with the juices of the meat and the fragrance of the spices — achieving a depth of flavour that no shortcut can replicate.

Mughal Origins

The origins of Biriyani trace back to the Mughal Empire, which ruled the Indian subcontinent from the 16th to the 18th century. The royal kitchens of the Mughals were home to extraordinary culinary innovation — blending Persian, Central Asian, and South Asian cooking traditions.

It is believed that the dum technique — sealing the pot with dough and cooking over low heat — was refined in these royal kitchens. The Kacchi method, in particular, is considered one of the most technically demanding, requiring precise timing so that the meat and rice finish cooking at exactly the same moment.

Kacchi Comes to Dhaka

As the Mughal Empire expanded, its culinary traditions spread across Bengal. Dhaka, then known as Jahangirnagar, became the capital of Bengal under the Mughals in 1610, and with it came the royal cooks and their recipes.

Over generations, local Dhaka cooks adapted the Kacchi recipe — incorporating local spices, Bengali mustard oil, and the fragrant kewra water (screwpine essence) that gives Dhaka's Kacchi its unmistakably floral aroma.

Old Dhaka: The Home of Kacchi

By the 19th and 20th centuries, Old Dhaka had become the undisputed home of Kacchi Biriyani in Bangladesh. Legendary establishments in areas like Nazimuddin Road, Chawk Bazar, and around the mosques of Lalbagh built their entire reputation on this one dish.

Kacchi became inseparable from Dhaka's major celebrations — weddings, Eid feasts, and family gatherings were not complete without it.

What Makes Dhaka Kacchi Different

Unlike Calcutta or Hyderabadi biryani, Dhaka's Kacchi is characterised by:

  • The use of young mutton — more tender and less gamey than older animals
  • Curd (yoghurt) marinade with raw papaya paste as a natural tenderiser
  • Heavy use of saffron and kewra for fragrance — the Dhaka signature
  • Fried potatoes (aloo) cooked alongside the meat — unique to Old Dhaka
  • The dough-sealed dum pot, preserving every drop of steam and flavour

A Living Tradition

Today, Kacchi Biriyani remains the most celebrated dish in Bangladesh. It is the first item ordered at weddings, the first dish cooked on Eid morning, and the meal most people associate with the taste of home.

At Dhaka Kacchi, we are proud to carry this tradition forward — using the same techniques, the same patience, and the same love that has defined this dish for centuries.

Weddings & Eid Tradition

Kacchi becomes the defining dish of celebration in Bangladesh — no wedding or Eid is complete without it. Families place orders days in advance.
